\section{Appendix A: Decoding the Baseline Posterior}
\label{app:posterior}

Probe~1 is trained on $\mathrm{sign}(\Delta U_t)$, a binary function of the
belief rather than the belief itself. This appendix specifies the analysis that
separates the two, and states precisely what its result licenses.

\subsection{A.1 Setup}

Fix a combination $c=(m,S)$ of model $m$ and scenario $S$. Sessions are indexed
by seed $s\in\mathcal{S}$, $|\mathcal{S}|=100$, and steps by $t\in\{1,\dots,T\}$,
$T=10$. For each step define

\begin{align*}
x_{s,t} &\in \mathbb{R}^{d_m}
  && \text{final-layer residual, last prompt token,}\\
y_{s,t} &= p(\theta\mid h_{s,t}) \in \Delta^{K_S-1}
  && \text{exact posterior, } K_S=|\Theta_S|,\\
u_{s,t} &= \Delta U_{s,t} \in \mathbb{R}
  && \text{baseline expected-utility margin.}
\end{align*}

$y$ is recomputed from the stored history by the environment's own posterior
map using only records preceding $t$; $u$ is recovered by inverting the
scenario's oracle link. $K_S=4$ for \textit{Trust Game}, \textit{Machine},
\textit{Chicken} and $K_S=3$ otherwise. \textit{Bandit} is excluded: its
hidden parameter is binary, so $y$ is one-dimensional and monotone in $u$, and
the test of \S A.4 is vacuous there. This leaves $63$ combinations of
$n=1000$ steps each.

Regressions are fitted \emph{within} a combination only, so posteriors of
different dimension are never reconciled and no quantity is pooled across
scenarios.

\subsection{A.2 Estimator}

Multi-output ridge regression, in dual form since $n\ll d_m$. For a training
index set with centered, scaled design $X\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{\mathrm{tr}}\times d_m}$
and targets $Y$,
\[
\hat A=\bigl(XX^{\!\top}+\lambda I\bigr)^{-1}\!\bigl(Y-\bar y\bigr),
\qquad
\hat Y_{\ast}=X_{\ast}X^{\!\top}\hat A+\bar y ,
\]
with $\lambda=\operatorname{tr}(XX^{\!\top})/n_{\mathrm{tr}}$, a scale-free
default so that $\lambda$ is comparable across models of differing width. The
map $x\mapsto\hat y$ is linear. The penalty is \emph{not} tuned; reported
values are therefore a lower bound on an optimised probe.

\subsection{A.3 Protocol}

Let $\{\mathcal{F}_1,\dots,\mathcal{F}_{10}\}$ partition $\mathcal{S}$ into ten
folds \emph{of seeds}. For each fold, with $\mathrm{tr}$ and $\mathrm{te}$ the
step sets induced by the complementary seed sets:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item Compute the mean and scale of $x$ on $\mathrm{tr}$; apply to both sets.
\item Let $e_1<\dots<e_{21}$ be the empirical quantiles of $\{u_i\}_{i\in\mathrm{tr}}$
      and $b(\cdot)$ the induced $20$-level bin map.
\item For each bin $\beta$, set $\bar y_\beta=\operatorname{mean}\{y_i : i\in\mathrm{tr},\,
      b(u_i)=\beta\}$, falling back to $\bar y_{\mathrm{tr}}$ when
      $|\{i\in\mathrm{tr}: b(u_i)=\beta\}|<5$.
\item Replace $y_i \leftarrow y_i-\bar y_{b(u_i)}$ for $i\in\mathrm{tr}\cup\mathrm{te}$.
\item Fit \S A.2 on $\mathrm{tr}$; predict on $\mathrm{te}$.
\end{enumerate}

Grouping by seed is required, not cosmetic: the $T$ steps of a session share a
hidden parameter and a history prefix, so a step-level split would leak session
identity into every fold.

Three quantities are computed, all on held-out folds: $R^2_{\mathrm{full}}$
(steps 2--4 omitted), $R^2_{\mathrm{partial}}$ (full protocol), and
$R^2_{\mathrm{shuf}}$ (full protocol with $y$ permuted across steps, fixing the
zero point empirically). $R^2$ is evaluated per output coordinate against the
\emph{training} mean,
$1-\sum_{\mathrm{te}}(y-\hat y)^2/\sum_{\mathrm{te}}(y-\bar y_{\mathrm{tr}})^2$,
then averaged over the $K_S$ coordinates --- the out-of-sample convention,
stricter than scoring against the test mean.

\subsection{A.4 No Leakage, and What a Positive Result Licenses}

\begin{proposition}[Fold independence]
For every fold, both the ridge coefficients $\hat A$ and the correction map
$\beta\mapsto\bar y_\beta$ are measurable with respect to the training data
alone. Test-fold observations enter only through evaluation.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The bin edges in step~2 are quantiles of $\{u_i\}_{i\in\mathrm{tr}}$; the bin
means in step~3 average $\{y_i\}_{i\in\mathrm{tr}}$; the standardisation in
step~1 uses training moments; and $\hat A$ in \S A.2 is a function of
$(X_{\mathrm{tr}},Y_{\mathrm{tr}})$ only. Step~4 applies the already-fitted map
$b$ and the already-fitted means $\bar y_\beta$ pointwise. No statistic of
$\mathrm{te}$ appears in any of these. \qedhere
\end{proof}

Consequently $R^2_{\mathrm{partial}}$ is a genuine held-out quantity, and its
positivity cannot be produced by the residualisation itself. Substantively:

\begin{quote}
$R^2_{\mathrm{partial}}>0$ implies $x$ carries information about
$p(\theta\mid h_t)$ that is not a function of $b(u)$.
\end{quote}

Two qualifications make this exact. First, the removed component is
$\mathbb{E}[y\mid b(u)]$, not $\mathbb{E}[y\mid u]$: a binned conditional mean
absorbs any measurable function of $u$ only up to the resolution of the
partition, here $20$ quantile levels. Second, the baseline-optimal action is
$\mathrm{sign}(u)$, and $b$ refines the sign partition except within the single
bin containing $0$; the removed component therefore subsumes the action label
outside that bin. Positive $R^2_{\mathrm{partial}}$ is thus posterior
information beyond the decision margin at $20$-bin resolution, which is the
claim made in the main text.

The interpretation rests on linearity. A linear map cannot accumulate log
likelihoods across ten rounds and renormalise them; if a linear readout of $x$
lands on $p(\theta\mid h_t)$, that computation was already performed by the
network.

All $63$ combinations are positive on the partial test; the largest shuffled
value anywhere in the grid is $0.001$. Weakest combinations:
\model{R1-Qwen} 7B on \textit{BoS} ($0.174$) and \model{Gemma-2} 2B on
\textit{Monty Hall} ($0.172$). Strongest: \model{Qwen-3.6} 27B and
\model{Llama-3.3} 70B on \textit{Machine} ($0.624$, $0.616$).

The three reactive scenarios retain $0.382$--$0.460$, comparable to
\textit{Chicken} ($0.470$) and above \textit{Monty Hall} ($0.300$): the
posterior over opponent types is represented about as well where the
belief-to-action mapping fails as where it succeeds. In those scenarios a
positive partial $R^2$ necessarily means the representation separates
hypotheses implying the \emph{same} action --- an opponent playing
\textsc{Tit-for-Tat} after the agent played \textsc{Stag} from one that always
plays \textsc{Stag} --- a distinction the decision margin cannot express, which
is why it survives residualisation.

\subsection{A.6 Scope}

We claim a substantial component of the posterior is linearly decodable, not
that the posterior is recovered: $0.787$ and $0.451$ are substantial, not
ceiling. Decodability is not use; whether the action selection reads this
representation is the question the steering intervention addresses. Values are
final-layer only, under $10$-fold seed-grouped cross-validation rather than the
leave-one-seed-out protocol used for the reported Probe~1 accuracies, and with
an untuned penalty.
